Gunite Pool Construction in McAllen, TX

A gunite pool is built shape by shape on your property, not dropped in as a pre-made shell — here's what that process actually looks like in the Rio Grande Valley.

Call Now for a Free Estimate Request a Quote Online

Gunite pool construction in McAllen starts the same way most concrete pools do anywhere: a design consultation, a permit pulled through the City of McAllen, and an excavation crew that digs the shape of your future pool into the ground. What's different here is what's under the topsoil — a lot of Rio Grande Valley lots sit over caliche, a dense chalky layer that slows excavation down and sometimes calls for heavier equipment than a standard dig.

Gunite is a dry concrete mix that gets hydrated at the nozzle and sprayed at high velocity over a steel rebar cage. Because it is placed under pressure rather than poured into forms, it packs into vertical walls and curved shapes that a form-and-pour job could never hold. That is the whole reason custom pools are built this way.

The Process

What Does Gunite Pool Construction Involve?

Layout and excavation come first. The pool outline is staked on the ground, checked against setbacks and easements, and dug to the design depth plus a few inches of over-excavation for the shell thickness. This is the stage caliche affects most.

Steel goes in next: a rebar cage tied at regular spacing, held off the soil so concrete can encase it completely on all sides. Plumbing lines for skimmers, returns, and main drains get roughed in and pressure-tested before anything is sprayed over them, because finding a bad joint after the shell is up is an expensive lesson.

The gunite shoot itself is a single day. Crews work the mix at a stiff two to three inch slump and a water-to-cement ratio in the 0.35 to 0.45 range, then carve and float the surface to the finished contour. The shell then cures. This is the step that gets rushed most often and matters most in our climate — concrete gains strength by hydrating, not by drying, and the American Shotcrete Association's guidance is seven continuous days of water curing, with wet-fogging of the fresh surface beforehand to limit shrinkage cracking.

After curing comes tile and coping, the interior finish, and decking. The shell is pressure washed before the finish goes on so the new material actually bonds to it. Electrical work is tied in and bonded to the requirements of Article 680 of the National Electrical Code, the pool is filled, and startup begins with daily brushing and close chemistry control while the new finish cures underwater.

Signs It's a Fit

When Do You Need Gunite Construction?

  • Your lot is an irregular shape, pinched by setbacks, or has a mature tree or slope you want the pool to work around rather than fight.
  • You want features built into the structure itself — a tanning ledge, swim-out bench, beach entry, or an attached spa that spills into the pool.
  • You need a specific depth profile, whether that is a shallow play area for young kids or a genuine deep end.
  • The pool, decking, outdoor kitchen, and landscaping are one project, so the elevations and drainage need to be designed together instead of retrofitted.
  • Your yard access is too tight to bring in a pre-formed shell on a truck or lift it over the house with a crane.
  • You plan to stay in the home long term and want a structure with a 50-year service life.
The Real Cause

Why Does Caliche Slow Down Excavation?

Caliche is soil that has been cemented together, usually by calcium carbonate leaching down through the profile and precipitating into a continuous layer. It is characteristic of arid and semiarid regions and shows up across the Southwest, including South Texas, and individual layers can exceed a meter in thickness.

Its hardness is unpredictable, which is the actual problem. Poorly cemented caliche is weak and crumbly and digs about like firm dirt. Well-cemented caliche, where the voids between grains are completely filled, is hard, dense, and durable enough to stop a standard excavator bucket cold. Two lots on the same McAllen street can differ, so the honest answer at the estimate stage is a range rather than a promise.

Caliche also blocks water from infiltrating downward. That matters after the pool is finished, not just during the dig, because runoff that cannot drain away collects in the soil around the shell and deck. Planning surface drainage properly from the start is what keeps that from becoming a cracked deck in McAllen a few years down the road.

Investment

What Affects the Cost of Gunite Pool Construction in McAllen?

Custom gunite pools in this market generally run $50,000 to $100,000, and where you land inside that range is driven by six things more than anything else.

Size and Depth

Every additional foot of depth adds excavation, steel, gunite, finish surface, and water. Depth costs more per square foot than length does.

Soil Conditions

Hard caliche means more machine hours and sometimes a breaker attachment. This is the single least predictable line item on a Valley lot.

Decking Scope

Deck square footage and material can swing the total by tens of thousands on its own, from broom concrete up to travertine.

Built-In Features

Attached spas, tanning ledges, water features, and automation each add plumbing runs, electrical, and structure.

Interior Finish

Plaster is the least expensive, quartz sits in the middle, and pebble costs most upfront while lasting the longest.

Site Access

A narrow side yard or a backyard reachable only over the house changes what equipment can be used and how long everything takes.

One cost most homeowners do not budget for: the interior finish is a wear item, not a permanent one. The shell lasts 50 years, but the plaster on it lasts 7 to 12, and quartz or pebble 10 to 20. Treating pool resurfacing in McAllen as a scheduled expense rather than a surprise is the difference between a maintained pool and a deferred one.

Choosing

Gunite vs. Fiberglass: Which Do You Need?

Choose gunite if the shape matters. Custom geometry, a specific depth profile, an integrated spa, or a lot that will not accommodate a rectangular shell are all reasons the extra weeks and dollars earn their keep. It is also the answer when access is too tight to deliver a pre-formed shell, since gunite arrives in a hose rather than on a flatbed.

Choose fiberglass pool installation in McAllen if speed and running cost matter more than shape. A fiberglass shell is swimmable in one to two weeks instead of six to ten, costs roughly half as much, and its non-porous surface holds up better against our very hard tap water than plaster does, since there is no calcium in the shell for aggressive water to leach out.

The deciding question is usually not budget but the yard. Walk out and look at how you would get a 16-foot-wide shell into the backyard. If there is no path and no crane position, gunite is the answer regardless of what you would have preferred on price.

Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a gunite pool take to build in McAllen?

Six to ten weeks from the first shovel to the first swim is the realistic range. Excavation and steel take about a week, the gunite shoot is a single day, curing runs at least a week after that, and then plumbing, interior finish, decking, and startup follow in sequence. Caliche on the lot, rain delays, and city inspection scheduling are the three things that most often push a McAllen build toward the longer end.

Why does gunite cost more than fiberglass?

A gunite shell is built from scratch on your property out of steel and sprayed concrete, then hand-finished, rather than arriving as a completed shell on a truck. You are paying for roughly six additional weeks of skilled labor across several trades. What that buys is design freedom, since the shell can be any shape, depth, or size your lot allows.

Can a gunite pool be any shape I want?

Effectively yes, and that is the main reason to choose it. Because the shell is formed with steel rebar and sprayed concrete instead of pulled from a factory mold, it can wrap around a mature tree, follow an angled property line, drop to a deep end wherever you want it, and include a tanning ledge, bench, or attached spa as part of the same monolithic structure.

Why does a gunite shell need to be kept wet after it is sprayed?

Concrete gains strength through hydration, not by drying out, so a shell that loses its moisture too fast ends up weaker and prone to shrinkage cracking. The American Shotcrete Association's guidance is that water is the best curing method and should run continuously for seven days. In McAllen, where summer highs average close to 100 degrees, that curing water matters more than it does almost anywhere else, and a crew that skips it is costing you shell strength you paid for.

Do I need a permit for a gunite pool in McAllen?

Yes. The City of McAllen has adopted the 2024 edition of the International Swimming Pool and Spa Code, and a pool permit is pulled before excavation begins. The application asks for pool dimensions, surface area, gallon capacity, and equipment location, and carries plan review and zoning fees. Note that a McAllen permit becomes invalid if work has not started within six months of issuance, so timing the permit to the build schedule matters.

How long does a gunite pool shell last?

The structural shell itself commonly lasts 50 years or more. What wears out is the interior finish on top of it, which needs replacing every 7 to 20 years depending on whether it is plaster, quartz, or pebble. Budgeting a gunite pool means treating the shell as a one-time cost and resurfacing as a recurring one.
